Board & seeds

The Pallanguzhi Board & Seeds

பல்லாங்குழி பலகையும் விதைகளும்

A good Pallanguzhi set is half the pleasure: the weight of a carved board, the click of seeds falling into wood. Here’s how a board is laid out and what to play with.

By Justin BenitoUpdated 27 Oct 20262 min read

Anatomy of a Pallanguzhi board

  • 14 playing pits (kuzhis) in two rows of seven. Each player owns the row nearest to them.
  • Store pits: many boards have a larger hollow at each end to hold captured seeds. On simpler boards players keep captures in a pile beside them.
  • Hinge: folding boards close like a box, with the seeds stored inside.

Board materials

Wood is the classic: rosewood, teak and jackfruit wood age beautifully. Carved boards from Chettinad are prized, and some are shaped like fish or decorated with brass corners and studs. Brass boards are heavier, ring when seeds drop, and last generations. Modern sets are often lacquered wood made by artisan cooperatives.

Choosing seeds

CounterWhat it isFeel
Manjadi மஞ்சாடிGlossy red seeds of Adenanthera pavoninaUniform, shiny, the classic look
Puliyankottai புளியங்கொட்டைTamarind seedsDark brown, flat, easy to find at home
Sozhi சோழிCowrie shellsSatisfying clatter, easy to count

You’ll need 70 counters for the standard 5-per-pit game, and a few spares. If you play with 6 or 12 per pit, you need 84 or 168.

No board at home? You can play on our online Pallanguzhi board and switch between manjadi, tamarind and sozhi from the menu.

Caring for a wooden board

Keep it out of direct sun and damp, wipe it with a dry cloth, and rub in a little coconut or linseed oil once or twice a year to keep the wood from drying out.
