Pallanguzhi Strategy: 9 Tips to Win

பல்லாங்குழி வெல்லும் யுக்திகள்

Pallanguzhi looks like luck until you start counting. These nine tips are what separate a beginner from someone who beats their grandmother, or at least makes her put down her coffee.

By Justin BenitoUpdated 27 Oct 20262 min read

1. Always count your landing pit

Every move is decided by where your last seed lands. From a pit with n seeds, your last seed lands n pits ahead (anticlockwise). Before you touch anything, count it out, then look at the next two pits after it.

2. Look for “empty, then full”

The capture pattern is: last seed → empty pit → pit you take. Scan the board for an empty pit sitting just before a big pile, then look for one of your pits whose seeds would land right before that empty pit.

3. Don’t leave your opponent a capture

After your move, check the board from your opponent’s side. Do they have a pit that lands right before an empty pit with a big pile behind it? If yes, try a different move, or fill that empty pit yourself.

4. Think in relays, not single sows

A move rarely stops after one handful. If your last seed lands next to a non-empty pit, the move continues. Strong players trace the whole chain: “I land here, scoop 4, those land there, the next pit is empty, so I capture...”. In our online game, hovering over a pit shows where your first handful lands.

5. Farm pasus on your own row

A pasu goes to the owner of the row, not the player sowing. Pits with 3 seeds on your row are opportunities: if your opponent’s sowing passes through, it becomes 4 and the pasu is yours.

6. Watch 3s on your opponent’s row

The reverse is also true. If your move will drop a seed into a 3 on your opponent’s row, you’re gifting them four seeds. Sometimes it’s worth it for a big capture. Usually it isn’t.

7. Big pits are both bombs and shields

A pit with 12+ seeds travels around the whole board. It can set up a massive relay, but while it sits there it’s also a juicy target. Keep big piles where your opponent can’t easily land just before an empty pit next to them.

8. Control the tempo near the end

The round ends when the player to move has an empty row, and remaining seeds go to the owner of each row. Near the end, keeping seeds on your own row is worth points. Avoid moves that shovel your last seeds onto your opponent’s side.

9. Practise against a stronger player

The fastest way to improve is to lose to someone better and notice why. Sheila Paati has three moods: Chellam (easy), Konjam serious (medium) and Paati mode, which looks many moves ahead.
